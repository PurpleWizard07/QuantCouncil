> 💡 **Key takeaway:** Every instrument exists to do a *specific job* — raise capital, provide income, transfer risk, or offer leverage. Knowing *what each is useful for* (and its hidden risks) matters more than memorizing definitions.

**Learning objectives:** distinguish the major asset classes; explain what each is *for*; read the key metrics (market cap, free float, NAV, yield, duration); understand derivatives as *tools*, not just bets.

## Equities (stocks)

A share is **partial ownership** of a company — a claim on its residual profits and assets.

- **Common stock:** voting rights + variable dividends; last in line if the company is liquidated, but unlimited upside. **Preferred stock:** priority on dividends/liquidation, usually no vote; behaves part-bond, part-equity.
- **Market capitalization** = price × shares outstanding — the market's total valuation of the equity.
- **Free float** = shares actually available to public trading (excludes promoter/locked holdings); used to weight [indices](/learn/indian-markets/indices-etfs-ipos#4-indices-nifty-sensex-banknifty-sectoral) and a driver of [liquidity](/learn/foundations/liquidity-volatility-correlation#9-liquidity).
- **Useful for:** owning business growth over time; the core of long-term [investing](/learn/foundations/investing-trading-speculation#4-investing-vs-trading-vs-speculation).

## ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)

A fund that holds a basket (usually tracking an index) and **trades like a stock**.

- **Creation/redemption:** authorized participants swap the underlying basket for ETF units (and vice versa), an arbitrage mechanism that keeps the ETF price near **NAV** (net asset value per unit).
- **Tracking error:** how far the ETF's return drifts from its index (fees, cash drag, imperfect replication).
- **Useful for:** cheap, liquid, diversified exposure (e.g. a NIFTY ETF, gold ETF). Watch spreads in thin ETFs; trade near NAV with limit orders.

## Bonds (fixed income)

A **loan** to a government/company that pays interest and returns principal at maturity.

- **Coupon:** the periodic interest. **Yield:** the return based on *current price* (rises when price falls — inverse relationship). **Duration:** sensitivity of price to interest-rate changes (longer duration → bigger price swings when rates move). **Credit risk:** chance the issuer defaults. **Interest-rate risk:** price falls when rates rise.
- **Useful for:** income, capital preservation, diversification (often — but not always — [less correlated](/learn/foundations/liquidity-volatility-correlation#correlation) with equities).

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Thinking bonds are "risk-free." They carry interest-rate, credit, and [inflation](/learn/foundations/return-compounding-inflation#8-inflation-nominal-vs-real-returns) risk.

## Commodities

Physical goods: energy (crude), metals (gold, copper), agriculture. Traded mostly via [futures](/learn/derivatives/futures-mechanics). **Non-cash-flowing** — value is pure supply/demand and storage/carry. **Useful for:** inflation hedging (debatable), diversification, and producer/consumer hedging.

## Currencies / Forex

Trading one currency against another (USD/INR, EUR/USD). Enormous, liquid, largely OTC; driven by interest-rate differentials, trade flows, and central-bank policy ([RBI](/learn/indian-markets/regulatory-stack-accounts-settlement)). **Useful for:** hedging currency exposure and macro speculation; retail FX is high-leverage and high-risk.

## Mutual funds

Pooled, professionally managed portfolios priced at **NAV** (once daily), not intraday. **Active** (manager picks; higher fees, usually [underperforms](/learn/foundations/price-drivers-and-market-efficiency#8-how-efficient-are-markets-really) after costs) vs **passive/index** (tracks a benchmark; low fees). **Useful for:** hands-off diversified investing (esp. index funds/SIPs).

## REITs / InvITs

**REITs** (real estate) and **InvITs** (infrastructure) let you own income-producing property/infra via exchange-traded units — passing through rent/toll income. **Useful for:** real-asset income exposure without buying property directly. Watch leverage, interest-rate sensitivity, and liquidity.

## Derivatives

Instruments whose value *derives* from an underlying. Covered in depth later; here's the map:

| Derivative | One-line | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Futures** | Obligation to buy/sell at a set price on a date | [Futures mechanics](/learn/derivatives/futures-mechanics) |
| **Options** | *Right* (not obligation) to buy/sell | [Options building blocks & Greeks](/learn/derivatives/options-building-blocks-and-greeks) |
| **Forwards** | Like futures but private/OTC, customizable, more counterparty risk | [Futures mechanics](/learn/derivatives/futures-mechanics) |
| **Swaps** | Exchange one cash-flow stream for another (e.g. fixed↔floating interest) | [Advanced Topics](/learn/advanced-topics/regimes-volatility-portfolio-optimization) |

- **Useful for:** **hedging** (transfer risk), **leverage** (control large notional with less capital), and **speculation**. The same tool insures a farmer *or* gambles a paycheck — the instrument is neutral; the [risk management](/learn/risk-management) is on you.

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** Match the *instrument* to the *job*: equities/ETFs for ownership and growth, bonds for income/preservation, commodities/FX for hedging and macro, funds/REITs for diversified exposure, derivatives for hedging and leverage. Every one has a *hidden* risk (liquidity, credit, rate, leverage) — name it before you trade it.
