A curated, quality-first list. The ordering principle throughout this course: **official exchange/regulator docs → academic papers → reputable institutions → established textbooks → high-quality educational sources.** Reddit, YouTube, Twitter/X threads, and "trading gurus" are **not** authoritative and are deliberately excluded. For anything about Indian fees, taxes, settlement, or regulation, **verify against the official sources below** — they supersede any summary (including this course). 🗓️

## 2. Books (established, by topic)

> Read *skeptically* — even good books contain opinions and dated claims. Prefer editions that discuss evidence.

**Foundations & investing**
- *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* — Burton Malkiel (markets efficiency, humility about beating them).
- *The Intelligent Investor* — Benjamin Graham (value investing; the "Mr. Market" and margin-of-safety ideas). Dated specifics, timeless temperament.
- *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits* — Philip Fisher (qualitative business analysis).

**Fundamental analysis & valuation**
- *Financial Statement Analysis* — Martin Fridson & Fernando Alvarez.
- *Investment Valuation* / *Damodaran on Valuation* — Aswath Damodaran (DCF, multiples; Damodaran's free online materials and datasets are outstanding).
- *The Little Book of Valuation* — Aswath Damodaran (accessible entry point).

**Risk, probability & trading psychology**
- *Fortune's Formula* — William Poundstone (the Kelly criterion, told as history).
- *Thinking, Fast and Slow* — Daniel Kahneman (the cognitive biases behind [trading psychology](/learn/psychology/bias-catalogue-and-discipline-systems)).
- *Fooled by Randomness* & *The Black Swan* — Nassim Taleb (luck, fat tails, and the danger of underestimating rare events — directly relevant to [why selling options can ruin you](/learn/derivatives/why-selling-options-is-not-free-money)).
- *Trading in the Zone* — Mark Douglas (trading discipline; treat as psychology, not strategy).

**Derivatives**
- *Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives* — John C. Hull (the standard graduate text; rigorous).
- *Option Volatility and Pricing* — Sheldon Natenberg (practitioner-focused; volatility and the Greeks).

**Quant, statistics & systematic trading**
- *Quantitative Trading* & *Algorithmic Trading* — Ernest Chan (accessible intro to systematic methods and their pitfalls).
- *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* — Marcos López de Prado (advanced; strong on the *dangers* of naïve ML and backtest overfitting — highly relevant to [backtesting pitfalls](/learn/backtesting-statistics/backtest-loop-and-pitfalls) and [quantitative trading](/learn/quantitative-trading/pairs-trading-and-cointegration)).
- *Trading Systems and Methods* — Perry Kaufman (encyclopedic reference on systematic methods).
- *Evidence-Based Technical Analysis* — David Aronson (applies statistical rigor and data-snooping correction to TA — the skeptical companion to [Technical Analysis](/learn/analysis/ta-honest-framing)).

**Market microstructure**
- *Trading and Exchanges* — Larry Harris (the definitive text on how markets/order books actually work; underpins [Market Microstructure](/learn/market-microstructure/order-flow-and-market-impact)).
- *Empirical Market Microstructure* — Joel Hasbrouck (advanced/academic).

**Market history & humility**
- *When Genius Failed* — Roger Lowenstein (LTCM; a masterclass in leverage and tail risk — see [Case Studies](/learn/case-studies/overfit-backtest-ltcm-retail-losses)).
- *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* — Edwin Lefèvre (classic trading memoir; entertaining, not a method).

## 3. Academic papers & foundational research

> Search on Google Scholar / SSRN / NBER. These shape the *evidence* behind factors and efficiency.

- Fama (1970), *Efficient Capital Markets* — the EMH statement.
- Fama & French (1992/1993) — the value and size factors; later the 5-factor model.
- Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) — **momentum** (returns continuation).
- Carhart (1997) — momentum as a 4th factor.
- Sharpe (1964) & the **CAPM**; Sharpe (1966/1994) — the Sharpe ratio.
- Black & Scholes (1973) and Merton (1973) — **option pricing**.
- Engle (1982) / Bollerslev (1986) — **ARCH/GARCH** volatility models (volatility clustering).
- Bailey & López de Prado — work on **backtest overfitting** and the "deflated Sharpe ratio" (why beautiful backtests mislead).
- Barber, Odean, and co-authors — retail traders **underperform** and trade too much (behavioral evidence echoed by **SEBI's Indian F&O studies**).

## 5. Python libraries

**Data & analysis**
- `pandas`, `numpy` — the workhorses for time series and math.
- `scipy`, `statsmodels` — statistics, regression, time-series tests (e.g. cointegration for [pairs trading](/learn/quantitative-trading/pairs-trading-and-cointegration)).
- `matplotlib`, `plotly` — charting equity curves and distributions.

**Backtesting & trading**
- `vectorbt`, `backtrader`, `zipline-reloaded`, `bt` — backtesting frameworks (each with trade-offs; learn one well and *always model costs/slippage*).
- `TA-Lib` / `pandas-ta` — technical indicators (remember [the skeptical framing](/learn/analysis/ta-honest-framing)).
- Broker SDKs — **Kite Connect** (`kiteconnect`), Upstox, Angel One SmartAPI — for live data/execution.

**Quant/ML (use with heavy skepticism)**
- `scikit-learn` — regression, trees, cross-validation (respect [overfitting](/learn/backtesting-statistics/backtest-loop-and-pitfalls)).
- `PyPortfolioOpt` — portfolio optimization/risk parity for [Advanced Topics](/learn/advanced-topics/regimes-volatility-portfolio-optimization).

<Callout type="info">
A library that makes it *easy* to backtest also makes it easy to **overfit**. Tools don't create edges; disciplined, out-of-sample, cost-aware process does.
</Callout>

## 6. Platforms & tools

- **Brokers/terminals (India):** compare on cost, reliability, and API quality — not on marketing. Use each broker's official charge sheet and brokerage calculator (see [the cost stack](/learn/indian-markets/cost-stack-and-stt)).
- **Paper-trading / simulators:** most brokers and platforms offer them — essential for [moving from paper → tiny capital → larger capital](/learn/practical-trading/capital-progression-broker-journaling). QuantCouncil's own paper-trading lab is built for exactly this stage.

## 7. How to evaluate *any* trading source (a meta-skill)

Before trusting a book, video, course, or "guru," ask:

- Do they distinguish **established facts from opinion**, and cite evidence?
- Do they emphasize **risk, costs, and drawdowns** — or only wins and lifestyle?
- Are results **out-of-sample and cost-adjusted**, or in-sample screenshots?
- Do they acknowledge that **most active traders lose** (the documented base rate)?
- Do they sell **certainty/urgency/guaranteed profits**? (🚩 the biggest red flag of all.)
- Would a **university textbook or a regulator** agree with the claim?

<Callout type="warning" title="Rule of thumb">
The louder the promise of easy, guaranteed, fast money, the more confidently you can ignore it. Real edges are modest, hard-won, risk-managed, and quietly discussed — never shouted with rocket emojis. This is the spirit of this entire course.
</Callout>
