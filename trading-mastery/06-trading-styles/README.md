# 06 — Trading Styles

> ⬅ Back to [README](../README.md) · Prev: [05 Technical Analysis](../05-technical-analysis/README.md) · Next: [07 Risk Management](../07-risk-management/README.md)

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** There is no "best" style — only the style that fits your **time, capital,
> temperament, and skill**, and whose edge survives **costs**. Faster styles face brutal
> [cost/slippage](../17-indian-markets/README.md#the-cost-stack) headwinds; slower styles demand
> patience and conviction. Pick one, master it, and *don't style-drift mid-loss*.

**Learning objectives:** compare the major styles on holding period, capital, skill, costs, and failure
modes; choose one deliberately.

---

## The comparison table

| Style | Holding period | Capital need | Skill/effort | Cost sensitivity | Main edge (hypothesis) | Typical failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Investing** | Years–decades | Low to start (SIP-able) | Patience, business analysis | Very low | Compounding + business growth | Impatience; buying at bubble prices; behavior in drawdowns |
| **Position trading** | Weeks–months | Moderate | Trend/fundamental read | Low | Riding medium-term trends | Overstaying; ignoring regime change |
| **Swing trading** | Days–weeks | Moderate | TA + risk mgmt | Moderate | Short-term structure/momentum | Overtrading; whipsaws in ranges |
| **Momentum trading** | Days–months | Moderate | Trend-following discipline | Moderate | Returns *continuation* (documented factor) | Crashes/reversals; crowding |
| **Intraday** | Minutes–hours (flat by close) | Higher (margins) | High; fast decisions | **High** | Intraday flow/patterns | Costs + slippage eat edge; tilt |
| **Scalping** | Seconds–minutes | High | Very high; execution | **Extreme** | Tiny, frequent micro-edges | Costs dominate; competes with HFT |
| **Mean reversion** | Varies | Moderate | Statistical | Moderate–high | Overreactions *revert* | Trends "break" the mean; tail losses |
| **Trend following** | Weeks–months+ | Moderate | Systematic discipline | Low–moderate | Persistent trends across assets | Long chop periods; many small losses ([+40% winner math](../07-risk-management/README.md#6-why-a-40-win-rate-can-win-and-a-70-win-rate-can-lose)) |
| **Breakout trading** | Varies | Moderate | Level/volume read | Moderate | Range→trend transitions | False breakouts (fakeouts) |
| **Event-driven** | Around events | Moderate | Research + speed | Moderate | Mispriced reactions to news/results | [Expectations already priced](../03-price-and-market-behavior/README.md#6-information-and-expectations); IV crush ([options](../10-options/README.md#4-the-greeks)) |
| **Statistical arbitrage** | Short–medium | High | Heavy quant | High | Relative mispricings ([pairs/cointegration](../14-quantitative-trading/README.md)) | Relationships break; crowding; leverage |
| **Market making** | Seconds | High + infra | Very high; tech | High | Earn the [spread](../01-how-markets-work/README.md#3-the-order-book-bid-ask-and-spread) providing liquidity | [Adverse selection](../15-market-microstructure/README.md); inventory risk |

## The trade-offs, stated plainly

- **Faster ≠ more profitable.** As holding period shrinks, **[costs and slippage](../17-indian-markets/README.md#the-cost-stack)**
  balloon relative to profit per trade, and you compete against professionals and
  [HFT](../15-market-microstructure/README.md). Scalping/intraday have the *highest* skill bar and the
  *thinnest* retail edge — the opposite of how they're marketed.
- **Slower styles trade the "cost problem" for a "patience/conviction problem"** — you must sit through
  drawdowns and avoid tinkering ([psychology](../11-trading-psychology/README.md)).
- **Momentum and mean reversion are opposite bets** and suit opposite regimes (see
  [regime](../05-technical-analysis/README.md#adx)) — many traders unknowingly mix them and get the
  worst of both.

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** **Style-drifting under stress** — entering as a "swing trade," then holding a
> loser for months calling it "investing," then panic-scalping to make it back. Each switch abandons
> the plan the trade was sized for. Decide the style *before* entry and let
> [risk management](../07-risk-management/README.md) enforce it.

---

## Choosing your style — honest questions

- **Time:** Can you watch screens all day (intraday) or only review evenings (swing/position/investing)?
- **Capital:** Small accounts are *crippled* by per-trade costs in fast styles; investing/position
  trading scale down better.
- **Temperament:** Do frequent small losses ([trend following](../07-risk-management/README.md#6-why-a-40-win-rate-can-win-and-a-70-win-rate-can-lose))
  or rare large losses (naive [option selling](../10-options/README.md#why-selling-options-is-not-free-money))
  bother you more? Choose a payoff shape you can *hold*.
- **Edge:** Do you have a *tested* ([out-of-sample, cost-adjusted](../12-backtesting-and-statistics/README.md))
  reason your chosen style makes money — or just a vibe?

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** For most people with jobs and modest capital, **slower styles (investing,
> position/swing) with strict risk management** are far more survivable than intraday/scalping — which
> the [retail base rate](../17-indian-markets/README.md#the-retail-fo-reality) confirms.

**✅ Ready to move on when you can:** match a style to a realistic capital/time/temperament profile and
name each style's edge *and* failure mode.
